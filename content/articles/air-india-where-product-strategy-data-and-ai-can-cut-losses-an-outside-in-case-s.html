01 · SummaryThe problem, and where to start

Air India Group lost ₹22,238 crore in FY26, more than twice the year before. Income fell about 9% while costs rose. Most of the damage came from outside the airline, because the Pakistan airspace ban, the Iran war, fuel prices and a weak rupee all struck in the same year.

No product team can reopen an airspace or set the price of jet fuel. A product team can decide where the airline earns its money, how cheaply it serves each passenger and how long that passenger stays loyal. I would start in four places.

  • Rebuild the long-haul network around southern hubs. Flights from Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Mumbai barely need Pakistan's airspace. Domestic flights would feed them, which is the Emirates hub-and-spoke model adapted to India.
  • Handle disruption automatically. The system rebooks, refunds and compensates passengers before they call, which cuts cost and keeps their loyalty.
  • Redesign the booking flow and extras around India's new free-seat rule. Bundles and upgrade bids would take the place of seat fees.
  • Move AI from answering questions to taking actions. Service comes first, then pricing and maintenance.
Expected impact
The three product ideas, plus loyalty, could be worth roughly ₹1,500–2,500 crore a year once fully running. That is illustrative, and section 4 shows the working. The network change is the larger prize, but it needs route-level data to size. Together they would close part of the gap, not all of it, but they sit in areas the airline fully controls.

02 · ContextWhere Air India stands

Tata Group took over Air India in January 2022. That September it launched Vihaan.AI, a five-year plan that aimed for at least 30% of the domestic market alongside better service, reliability and technology. Air India merged with Vistara on 12 November 2024 and folded Club Vistara's roughly 4.5 million members into a single programme, Maharaja Club.

The fleet is changing slowly. A $400 million retrofit has finished the 27 older A320neos, but supplier delays have pushed the last 777-300ERs to October 2028. Reliability has improved but still trails the leader. DGCA data for June 2026 put the group's domestic on-time performance at 85.9%, against IndiGo's 89.4%. In May the gap was wider, at 74.5% against 82.8%. Internationally, Cirium ranked Air India fourth in the world for punctuality in June 2026.

Leadership has also changed. Tewolde Gebremariam, who previously ran Ethiopian Airlines, became chief executive on 1 October 2026 and has set up a cost committee. The airline also has one strong digital asset already. Its AI assistant, AI.g, handles about 40,000 queries a day. According to Microsoft, it has resolved more than 13 million conversations and passes only 3% to a human agent.

04 · RecommendationsStrategy first, then product by product

Strategy

A southern hub-and-spoke network

Largest prize · needs route data
The problem

Air India's long-haul network starts mainly in Delhi, the city the airspace ban hurts most. Westbound flights now detour over the Arabian Sea, and some North America flights stopped for fuel in Vienna or Copenhagen. Air India has also cut several routes, including Delhi to Vienna and Delhi to Birmingham.

The Emirates lesson

Emirates flies every route through one hub, Dubai. Short routes feed the hub, and the hub fills the long-haul aircraft. In 2025–26 it carried 53.2 million passengers and made a record $6.2 billion before tax. India cannot copy Dubai's state backing or costs, but it can copy the discipline of sending each aircraft where it earns most.

What I would build

I would start more long-haul flights from Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Mumbai, because their routes west barely touch Pakistan's airspace. Air India and Air India Express would feed those flights from the north and from Tier 2 cities. The product work is the connection itself, with one ticket, bags checked through to the final destination, protected connections and a simple transfer.

One honest limit

Air India studied a refuelling stop in Mumbai or Ahmedabad for Delhi to North America flights. In June 2026 it said payload studies favoured a corridor over Mongolia instead. A stop that only refuels does not pay. A hub where new passengers board does pay, because the stop earns revenue.

How I would measure it

I would track the share of passengers who connect, the misconnection rate, revenue per long-haul flight from the southern gateways and market share won back from foreign airlines on India to Europe routes. Navi Mumbai helps too. Moving Air India Express flights there frees slots at the main Mumbai airport for long-haul banks.

Product

Disruption handling and refunds

₹150–300 cr a year · illustrative
The problem

When a flight is cancelled, passengers phone, queue and wait. Refunds were about a fifth of DGCA complaints in several 2025 months. The refund rules from March 2026 shorten the timelines, so a slow refund is now a regulatory risk as well as a reputational one.

What I would build

The system rebooks passengers before they ask. A WhatsApp or push message offers two or three options, so the passenger can take a new flight, a refund or a voucher with one tap. When the rules require compensation, the system calculates and pays it automatically.

How I would measure it

I would track the share of disrupted passengers who resolve the problem without a human, the median time to a refund and repeat bookings within 90 days. The value comes from fewer calls and penalties. Retained loyalty is the bigger prize, but I cannot size it from outside.

Product

Booking flow and extras

₹600–900 cr a year · illustrative
The problem

The rule that 60% of seats must be free to select removes part of a reliable fee. Airlines have warned they will raise base fares to make up for it, which would hurt Air India's headline price.

What I would build

Bundles that fit the trip would replace single fees. A family flying Delhi to London would see seats and bags together, and a business traveller would see lounge access and flexibility. I would add upgrade bids for premium seats that would otherwise fly empty, and measure every step of the booking flow.

How I would measure it

I would track revenue from extras per passenger, conversion at each booking step and the take-up of each bundle. A 1% lift on ₹71,870 crore of income is about ₹700 crore a year.

Product

Maharaja Club

₹200–400 cr a year · illustrative
The problem

Air India merged two loyalty programmes in 2024 and changed its award chart in April 2026. Members who feel their points have lost value will book with other airlines.

What I would build

Each member would see a clear, personal view of what their points can buy. I would grow earning through co-brand cards and Tata Group brands, and send targeted offers to members on the routes the detours hit hardest.

How I would measure it

I would track partner revenue, the share of bookings from active members and churn among top-tier members. Partner revenue is the main lever, but I cannot see the contracts.

Data

Crew and fleet scheduling

Protection against a very bad month
The problem

In December 2025, IndiGo misjudged how many pilots it needed under new duty-time rules. It paid ₹22.76 crore in cancellation compensation that month, about four times what it had paid in the previous eleven years. Air India was far less affected, but the lesson applies to every airline.

What I would build

A planning tool would test every schedule against duty rules, expected sick leave and the weather season before the schedule is published. On the day of a disruption, the same tool would suggest the crew and aircraft swaps that cancel the fewest flights.

How I would measure it

I would track cancellations caused by crew shortages, the cost of each disruption and the time to recover the network.

AI

From answers to actions

₹550–900 cr a year · illustrative
The problem

AI.g answers questions well, but answering is the easy part. Most of the value lies in completing tasks for passengers and in decisions passengers never see.

What I would build

I would build in three stages. First, AI.g would complete tasks such as changing a flight or adding a bag. Second, pricing models would forecast demand by day and choose each day's long-haul routing. Third, maintenance models would predict part failures from aircraft data.

How I would measure it

I would track tasks completed per conversation, unit revenue against forecast and unplanned technical delays. A 1% gain in yield is worth about ₹700 crore a year.

05 · RoadmapTwelve to eighteen months, in three phases

The order matters. AI needs clean, connected data, so the platform work comes before the AI work. The network change starts with a study, because a hub is expensive to undo.

06 · HonestyRisks and what I would need to check

Writing from outside means much of this rests on assumptions. Before building anything, I would want answers to these questions.

  1. Which routes, by origin and destination, would actually fill long-haul flights from a southern hub, and what the hub would cost in slots and ground handling.
  2. The real drop-off rates in the booking flow, which no outsider can see.
  3. Current revenue from extras per passenger, and how much of it the free-seat rule removed.
  4. The true cost of disruption today, including compensation, hotels and call-centre time.
  5. How far the merged systems of Air India, Vistara and Air India Express share data.
  6. Which of these ideas Air India already has under way. The airline may be ahead of me on several.
